# Binary resolve-rate vs graded / partial-credit scoring for agent evaluation

Scope: 2023–2026 work found by web search and verified by fetching abstracts/full text. Every claim links to the source I read; where a number came only from a secondary summary I say so.

## 1. Head-to-head comparisons of binary vs graded metrics

- **TDAG / ItineraryBench (arXiv:2402.10178, 2024)** is the cleanest same-benchmark A/B I found: §5.4 reports that with binary scoring "in challenging tasks with a low probability of completion, binary scoring fails to distinguish between different methods effectively," while fine-grained averages spread across 42.85 (P&E), 43.02 (ReAct), 43.68 (P&S), 44.74 (ADAPT), 49.08 (TDAG). Binary values live only in Figure 3, which I could not read as text — treat the commonly quoted "27–32%" as unverified. [arXiv:2402.10178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10178)
- **Parallel WebBench (arXiv:2606.20724, 2026)** trains GRPO web agents and shows the signals diverge: completion rises 50.7% → 96.0% while element-wise F1 rises only 0.2489 → 0.4529, with "binary accuracy remain[ing] far below completion." [arXiv:2606.20724](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20724)
- **"Don't Pass@k: A Bayesian Framework for LLM Evaluation" (arXiv:2510.04265, ICLR 2026)** removes the either/or: it models outcomes as *categorical, not just 0/1*, with a Dirichlet prior, giving closed-form posterior mean/uncertainty "for any weighted rubric," faster convergence and greater rank stability than Pass@k at far smaller sample counts, explicitly "unif[ying] binary and non-binary evaluation." [arXiv:2510.04265](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04265)
- **DeepRed CTF benchmark (AIware 2026, DOI 10.1145/3805760.3814926)** replaces solved/unsolved with challenge-specific checkpoints; the best of ten commercially accessible LLMs reaches only **35% average checkpoint completion** — a score that does not exist under a binary metric. [AIware 2026 abstract](https://2026.aiwareconf.org/details/aiware-2026-benchmark---dataset-track/5/Do-Agents-Dream-of-Root-Shells-Partial-Credit-Evaluation-of-LLM-Agents-in-Capture-th)
- **CAFE (arXiv:2607.10380, 2026)** scores on a shared rubric and uses mixed-effects models to report **effect sizes, significance, and judge-human reliability** for component-level differences. [arXiv:2607.10380](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10380)
- **Sample-size context for the binary metric:** a replay analysis of SWE-bench, AppWorld and tau-bench (arXiv:2607.12338, 2026) finds that reproducing the *completed* benchmark's pairwise decisions at a strict 0-point threshold on a 5-point grid needs 15% of AppWorld tasks, 25% of tau-bench, and **90% of SWE-bench Verified**, with SWE-bench Lite not converging by 95%. [arXiv:2607.12338](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12338)

## 2. What binary hides — and where graded metrics fail

- **Binary hides behaviour:** AgentLens (arXiv:2605.12925, 2026) evaluated 2,614 OpenHands trajectories from eight backends on 60 SWE-bench Verified tasks and found **10.7% of passing trajectories are "Lucky Passes"** (regression cycles, blind retries, missing verification), per-model Lucky rates of 0.5%–23.2%, and models moving **up to five rank positions** when ranked by process quality instead of pass rate. [arXiv:2605.12925](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12925)
- **Binary overstates capability:** SWE-bench+ (arXiv:2410.06992, 2024) manually screened SWE-Agent+GPT-4 successes: 32.67% involved solution leakage, 31.08% were suspicious due to weak tests, and filtering dropped resolution from **12.47% to 3.97%**. [arXiv:2410.06992](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06992)
- **Binary is wrong on its own terms:** "Are 'Solved Issues' in SWE-bench Really Solved Correctly?" (arXiv:2503.15223, 2025) uses differential patch testing to find **7.8% of patches count as correct while failing the developer test suite**, 29.6% induce behavior different from the gold patch, and the combined weaknesses **inflate reported resolution rates by 6.2 absolute percentage points**. [arXiv:2503.15223](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15223)
- **Graded is reward-hackable:** PartHackBench stress-tests partial-credit evaluators with equal-terminal-progress trajectory pairs. On 15 matched held-out tasks the historical evaluator showed mean **Δhack = 0.252**, conditional ASR 10/15, end-to-end yield 10/18, and **rollback detection 0/14** (vs 14/14 for a current-state scorer). [PartHackBench README](https://github.com/HongyeYangGT/PartHackBench)
- **Rubric scores can be miscalibrated against ground truth:** RubricForge (arXiv:2608.13564, 2026) reports its edge over a generic G-Eval judge is **not statistically significant (McNemar p = 0.248)** and absolute-score calibration marginally favors the generic judge (|err| difference −0.048, p = 2×10⁻⁴), even though it over-credits failures half as often (**0.115 vs 0.173 false-pass rate**). [arXiv:2608.13564](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13564)
- **Graded-as-RL-reward drifts:** Rubric Dropout (arXiv:2608.11669, 2026) shows the training judge's score keeps climbing while a stronger gold judge peaks then *falls* by 3 points on HealthBench-Hard and 22 points on ResearchQA — reward hacking, the authors argue, not judge noise. [arXiv:2608.11669](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11669)
- **The grading scale itself changes conclusions:** "Grading Scale Impact on LLM-as-a-Judge" (arXiv:2601.03444, 2026) finds scale choice substantially shifts human-LLM agreement on six benchmarks even when within-group panel reliability is high, with **0–5 yielding the strongest alignment** and pooled reliability masking benchmark heterogeneity. [arXiv:2601.03444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03444)
- **Practitioner argument for tool-call partial credit:** the tau2-partial AgentBeats entry argues all-or-nothing evaluation gives "no signal about what they did correctly"; it reports 83.3% pass rate on its partial-credit variant — a claim, not a controlled study. [tau2-partial](https://agentbeats.dev/sulbhajain/tau2-partial)

## 3. SWE-bench-style partial-credit proposals

- **FeatureBench (arXiv:2602.10975, ICLR 2026):** Claude 4.5 Opus reaches **74.4% resolved on SWE-bench but only 11.0%** on 200 feature-oriented executable tasks. Not partial credit, but it shows what single-PR resolve rate hid. [arXiv:2602.10975](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10975)
- **SWE-smith (arXiv:2504.21798, 2025)**, **SWE-Gym (arXiv:2412.21139, ICML 2025)** and **R2E (ICML 2024)** scale tasks, environments and verifiers (50k instances, 40.2% resolve; 2,438 instances, 32.0%/26.0%) but **all still report binary resolve rate** — none proposes a partial-credit metric. [SWE-smith](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21798) · [SWE-Gym](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.21139) · [R2E](https://r2e.dev/)
- **AgentBoard (arXiv:2401.13178, NeurIPS 2024)** motivates a **fine-grained progress rate** because "current evaluation frameworks mostly focus on the final success rate, revealing few insights during the process." The canonical benchmark-side partial-credit metric. [arXiv:2401.13178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13178)
- **"Beyond Resolution Rates: Behavioral Drivers of Coding Agent Success and Failure" (arXiv:2604.02547, 2026)** analyses 9,374 trajectories from 19 agents on 500 tasks and shows patch complexity does not explain difficulty (12 never-solved tasks needed only simple patches); it argues for trace analysis, not a specific partial-credit number. [arXiv:2604.02547](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02547)

## 4. Process reward / step-level partial credit

- **Agent-as-a-Judge (arXiv:2410.10934, 2024)** introduces agentic intermediate evaluation over 55 DevAI tasks with 365 hierarchical requirements and reports it "dramatically outperforms LLM-as-a-Judge and is as reliable as our human evaluation baseline." [arXiv:2410.10934](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10934)
- **AgentProcessBench (arXiv:2603.14465, 2026)** supplies 1,000 trajectories and 8,509 human-labeled steps with **89.1% inter-annotator agreement**, a ternary label scheme and an error-propagation rule. Process signals "provide complementary value to outcome supervision, significantly enhancing test-time scaling"; weaker policies show *inflated* correct-step ratios via early termination. [arXiv:2603.14465](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14465)
- **Counter-evidence on predictive validity:** "Credit Without Ground Truth" (arXiv:2608.19760, 2026) audits step-level credit in ALFWorld against executed-replay ground truth and finds **none shows reliable incremental fidelity beyond its own marginal-matched shuffled control**; implicit credit echoes policy fluency (median rank correlation +0.75), outcome conditioning adds no causal information (partial correlation −0.004), and no arm of a seven-arm pre-registered training experiment reliably beat the untrained policy. [arXiv:2608.19760](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19760)
- **Supporting evidence:** progress-advantage work (arXiv:2606.26080, 2026) derives an annotation-free log-probability-ratio step signal that beats confidence baselines and dedicated trained reward models across five benchmarks and four model families. [arXiv:2606.26080](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26080)
- **Related:** TRAIL (arXiv:2505.08638, 2025) finds the best long-context model scores only 11% at localizing agent errors; SWE-TRACE (arXiv:2604.14820, 2026) uses a rubric PRM to replace sparse outcome rewards; CRM (arXiv:2509.26578, 2025) conditions step rewards on the final outcome. [TRAIL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.08638) · [SWE-TRACE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14820) · [CRM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26578)

## 5. Reliability / agreement statistics

- **"Measurement Without Validity" (arXiv:2608.00794, 2026)** gives a three-layer multiplicative validity model (V_total ≤ V₁×V₂×V₃; three stages at 70% yield ≤34%), a survey of 55 agentic-evaluation papers finding **~82% apply structurally mismatched, incomplete, or absent inter-rater reliability metrics**, and thresholds — **ICC ≥ 0.70; α ≥ 0.67/0.70/0.80 by consequence level**. [arXiv:2608.00794](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00794)
- **ICC for graded scales:** the grading-scale study above uses **intraclass correlation coefficients** for absolute agreement and shows pooled reliability can mask subgroup heterogeneity. [arXiv:2601.03444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03444)
- **Intra-rater reliability is the binding problem for graded LLM judges:** "Rating Roulette" (arXiv:2510.27106, EMNLP 2025 Findings) shows low intra-rater reliability across runs, making scores "almost arbitrary in the worst case." [arXiv:2510.27106](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.27106)
- **McNemar for binary judge comparison:** RubricForge uses **McNemar (p = 0.248)** against a baseline judge, alongside Spearman for ranking graded outcomes. [arXiv:2608.13564](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13564)
- **AUROC appears for predicting trajectory success, not metric-type choice:** trajectory-adapted uncertainty quantification reports AUROC for predicting greedy-trajectory success; I found no study using AUROC or Kendall tau to *compare* binary vs graded metric choice. [Uncertainty quantification paper](https://arxivlens.com/paperview/details/beyond-single-turn-confidence-trajectory-adapted-uncertainty-quantification-for-llm-agents-2414-9f5bc937)

## NOT FOUND

- **No controlled head-to-head study** scoring the *same* trajectories under both binary resolve-rate and a partial-credit variant and reporting variance, power, sample-size savings, or effect sizes for the metric comparison itself. TDAG §5.4, WebBench, DeepRed, PartHackBench and the Bayesian framework approach this from different angles but none isolates the metric as the treatment.
- **"ABC (Agent Benchmark Correctness)"** — no such paper or benchmark found. **A paper titled "Measuring Coding Agents Beyond Resolve Rate"** — not found; closest is "Beyond Resolution Rates" (arXiv:2604.02547).
- **SWE-bench Verified "pass rate" vs "test coverage"** as a named published metric pair — not found; no official alternate partial-credit metric located. **"% of FAIL_TO_PASS tests passed" / "partial fix rate" as a formally proposed and validated metric** — not found either; only critiques of resolved-rate accuracy (SWE-bench+, 2503.15223).
- **OpenAI's "why we no longer evaluate SWE-bench Verified"** — HTTP 403 to my fetch (English and zh-Hant). Link seen in search results only; no claims made from it.
- **Evidence that graded metrics *compress* discriminative signal** (lower spread/power vs binary) with numbers — not found. Documented graded-scoring failures are over-crediting, reward hacking, calibration error and training-dose confounds, not compression.
- **Cohen's kappa used specifically to contrast binary vs graded agent benchmarks** — not found; kappa appears only in generic IRR discussion. **Kendall tau for graded agent-metric reliability** — not found. AgentProcessBench reports "89.1% inter-annotator agreement" without naming the statistic.
- **R2E, SWE-smith and SWE-Gym as partial-credit metric proposals** — all three are environment/data/training contributions reporting binary resolve rate.
